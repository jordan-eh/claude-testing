# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Knowledge workers and team leads who schedule meetings and have some awareness that their organization has too many of them. Likely managers, chiefs of staff, engineering leads, and heads of product. They're not anti-meeting ideologues — they're pragmatic people who feel the cost of calendar overload and want a tool that gives them cover to say no.

## Product Purpose
Debrief scores meeting invites against a rubric that asks: does this actually need to happen in real time? It surfaces a verdict ("Send an email" or "Schedule it") and a numeric score, then drafts the alternative if async wins. Success looks like users eliminating a measurable fraction of their recurring meetings within the first month.

## Brand Personality
Opinionated, direct, slightly irreverent. The brand takes a clear stance (most meetings are waste) and doesn't hedge. Three words: confident, pragmatic, blunt.

## Anti-references
Generic SaaS landing pages with soft gradients and "streamline your workflow" copy. Notion-clone aesthetics. Meeting tool brands that feel corporate and formal (Microsoft Teams, Zoom). Anything that looks like it was made with a page builder.

## Design Principles
1. Say the quiet part loud. The product has an opinion; the design should too.
2. Earn trust through specificity. Vague claims undermine a tool that promises precision.
3. Confidence without arrogance. Bold moves, but not aggressive.
4. Respect the reader's time. If a word doesn't earn its place, cut it.
5. Async-first applies to the design too. Don't over-explain what the mockup already shows.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
No stated WCAG level. Standard web accessibility expected.
